- This is a question answered on the web, never an action: penrose does not install, open or sign in to
  transfer, bank or wallet apps, and never sends money (`install_app` refuses these packages). The
  answer is a comparison the user acts on themselves.
- Today's rate: a Google search for "<from> to <to>" (for example "USD to GHS") shows the mid-market rate
  and its time at the top; quote it with the time. A provider's rate is lower by its margin: the fair
  comparison is the amount received for a fixed amount sent, fees included.
- Quotes without logging in: Wise `https://wise.com/gb/send-money/` (pick the currencies; the calculator
  shows fee, rate and the amount received), Remitly `https://www.remitly.com/` (choose the destination
  country; the rate shows on the page), Western Union `https://www.westernunion.com/` (Send money →
  estimate), WorldRemit `https://www.worldremit.com/` (the calculator on the country page), Xoom
  `https://www.xoom.com/` (enter the amount). Monito (`https://www.monito.com/`) compares many at once
  and names the cheapest for the amount; use it as one source, not the only one.
- Receiving rails decide which services count. Much of Africa receives into mobile money (M-Pesa in
  Kenya and Tanzania, MTN MoMo in Ghana, Uganda, Rwanda and Cameroon, Airtel Money, Orange Money), and
  the Africa-corridor apps (Sendwave, Taptap Send, LemFi, Chipper Cash, NALA) are often cheapest and
  fastest there, besides Wise, Remitly and WorldRemit; include them for an African destination. Brazil
  receives by PIX, India by UPI or a bank account (Wise, Remitly, Xoom, Western Union), Mexico and the
  Philippines by bank, cash pickup or wallet (GCash, Maya), the EU by SEPA, the US by bank or Zelle.
- Read two or three provider pages, note for each the fee, the rate, the amount received and the
  delivery time, and report them side by side with the time of the quote; say which receiving method
  each assumes (mobile money, bank account, cash pickup).
